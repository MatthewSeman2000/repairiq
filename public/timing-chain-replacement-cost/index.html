Timing Chain Replacement Cost

Timing chain replacement is one of the most expensive engine repairs, typically costing $900–$2,500. Unlike timing belts, chains are metal and designed to last the engine's lifetime — but certain engines are notorious for premature chain wear, often linked to oil maintenance habits.

Average Cost

  • 4-cylinder engine: $900–$1,600
  • V6 engine: $1,200–$2,200
  • V8 or dual-cam engine with multiple chains: $1,500–$2,500+
  • Labor: 6–15 hours depending on engine complexity

Engines Notorious for Timing Chain Problems

  • GM 3.6L V6 (Cadillac, Buick, Chevy, GMC) — stretched chains at 80,000–120,000 miles
  • GM 5.3L/6.2L V8 with AFM/DFM — lifter and chain wear from oil degradation
  • Ford 5.4L 3-valve V8 — chain tensioner failure
  • Ford EcoBoost 2.0T and 1.6T — early chain wear
  • BMW N47/N57 diesel — rear-mounted chain notorious for failure
  • Chrysler 3.6L Pentastar V6 — chain stretch at high mileage
  • Toyota/Lexus 3.5L 2GR — internal water pump failure accelerates chain wear

Symptoms of a Worn Timing Chain

  • Rattling noise on cold start that fades after warm-up (chain slap)
  • Check engine light with P0011, P0014 (camshaft position codes)
  • Rough idle or misfires
  • Reduced power and poor fuel economy
  • Engine won't start (chain has skipped teeth — serious)

Why Oil Changes Are Critical

Timing chain wear is almost always caused by oil degradation. Old, dirty oil loses its viscosity and fails to lubricate chain components properly. Chain tensioners rely on oil pressure to maintain proper tension. Extended oil change intervals are the number one cause of premature timing chain failure. Use full synthetic oil and change it at or before the manufacturer's recommended interval.

What's Included in Timing Chain Replacement

  • Timing chain (sometimes multiple)
  • Chain tensioners and guides
  • VVT sprockets and solenoids (often worn by same time)
  • Oil change (always done at same service)
  • Gaskets and seals for the timing cover
