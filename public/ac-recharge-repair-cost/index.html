AC Recharge and Repair Cost

A basic AC recharge costs $100–$350, but full AC system repairs can range from $150 to over $1,200 depending on what failed. AC problems are most noticeable in summer — and search volume for 'AC repair near me' spikes significantly in hot months.

Cost by Service Type

  • AC recharge (refrigerant only): $100–$200
  • Leak detection + recharge: $150–$350
  • AC compressor replacement: $500–$1,200
  • Condenser replacement: $400–$900
  • Evaporator replacement: $600–$1,200 (dashboard removal often required)

Why Does AC Stop Working?

  • Refrigerant leak (most common) — the system has no moving parts when off, so leaks are the #1 cause
  • Failed AC compressor
  • Clogged or damaged condenser (front of car, often hit by road debris)
  • Failed expansion valve or orifice tube
  • Electrical fault (pressure switch, relay, or wiring)

The Refrigerant Issue

Older vehicles (pre-1995) used R-12 refrigerant which is no longer manufactured and very expensive. Vehicles from 1995–2021 use R-134a. Newer vehicles (2022+) increasingly use R-1234yf, which is more environmentally friendly but costs significantly more — R-1234yf refrigerant alone can run $50–$100 per pound vs. $5–$15 for R-134a.

Don't Just 'Recharge' Without Finding the Leak

If your AC stopped cooling, simply recharging without finding and fixing the leak means the new refrigerant will leak out again — possibly within weeks. A proper repair includes leak detection, fixing the source, and then recharging.
